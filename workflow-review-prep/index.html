What to bring

Do not overprepare. Bring the morning as it is.

The point is not to impress anyone with a deck. The point is to see the current process clearly enough to scope one narrow pilot that the team would actually use.

01

The final output

The spreadsheet, email, dashboard screenshot, PDF, or deck people actually use in the morning.

02

The source reports

PMS exports, pickup files, forecasts, rate shops, channel reports, and recurring inbox or folder sources.

03

The manual steps

Copy and paste, formulas, edits, formatting, checks, comments, screenshots, and exception notes.

04

The timing rules

When files arrive, when the brief is needed, and what happens when something is missing or late.

05

The owner chain

Who assembles it, who reviews it, who acts on it, and who gets blamed when it is wrong.

06

The first win

The smallest reliable output that would make the team say, "we should not go back."

The review flow

The first 20 minutes should feel like opening the curtains.

01

Trace the morning.

We walk from source arrival through assembly, review, delivery, and the decision the brief is supposed to support.

02

Name the reliability risks.

Late files, wrong dates, duplicate attachments, manual edits, stale formulas, and unsupported AI notes get surfaced before build talk.

03

Pick one pilot.

The right starting point is narrow enough to ship and important enough that the team feels relief the next morning.

04

Define proof.

We leave with the source layer, run log, output shape, caveat rules, handoff needs, timeline, and scope boundary.
